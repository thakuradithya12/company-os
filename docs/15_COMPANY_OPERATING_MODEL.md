# 15 — Company Operating Model

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how the future company turns a founder's objective into useful online services, opportunities, client relationships, and deliverables. It describes business workflows and policy boundaries, not the number or classification of agents.

## 2. Operating concept

The company is a founder-controlled digital business. Its AI workforce performs research, operational coordination, communication preparation, digital production, verification, and delivery through registered capabilities. The founder sets strategy, service boundaries, budgets, pricing authority, and approval rules.

The company may operate two connected value streams:

1. **Information and niche intelligence:** identify useful information in a niche, verify it, organize it for a defined audience, and distribute it through approved online destinations.
2. **Digital work and delivery:** identify business needs, audit public websites or digital presence, propose suitable online work, develop approved deliverables, verify them, and hand them over under agreed terms.

The service catalogue is extensible. A new category is added only when it has a defined customer need, delivery process, quality criteria, pricing approach, and risk review.

## 3. Business lifecycle

### Stage A — Define direction
The founder supplies the target niche, audience, geography, company objective, budget, allowed sources, time horizon, quality bar, and desired output. The company records these as a versioned objective.

### Stage B — Research and qualify
The system researches permitted sources and returns a report containing findings, citations, freshness, uncertainty, and recommended opportunities. Business opportunities must include evidence of a specific need; an absence of a website or a weak audit score alone is not proof that a business wants to buy.

### Stage C — Prepare outreach
Create an opportunity record and a tailored message or call plan based only on checked facts. Check channel eligibility, suppression status, applicable requirements, and founder policy. Drafting is allowed before communication capability is enabled; actual sending/calling remains gated.

### Stage D — Discover requirements
Record the prospect's needs, goals, constraints, current systems, audience, deadline, budget range when voluntarily provided, decision process, and acceptance criteria. Do not pretend that assumptions are confirmed requirements. The prospect must be able to correct misunderstandings.

### Stage E — Prepare and negotiate proposal
Generate a versioned proposal containing scope, deliverables, exclusions, schedule assumptions, price and currency, payment milestones if applicable, revision limits, support terms, dependencies, risks, and acceptance method. Negotiation must stay within the founder's approved price/discount/scope/commitment limits. Any exception is escalated for approval.

### Stage F — Confirm deal
Record how acceptance was obtained, the accepted proposal version, the identity/authority representation available, timestamp, contract or written confirmation reference, and unresolved conditions. A summary, friendly reply, or verbal “sounds good” is not automatically proof of a binding contract. The applicable legal/contract process must be reviewed for the business.

### Stage G — Deliver project
Convert the accepted scope into an immutable baseline brief and a work plan. Each change to scope, deadline, price, or acceptance criteria requires a change request and any necessary client/founder approval. Production tasks are assigned through the shared task system.

### Stage H — Verify and hand over
Deliver preview links, source/artifact references, test evidence, credentials-transfer instructions through a secure process if applicable, known limitations, and acceptance steps. Never include secrets in ordinary chat or logs. Production deployment and public handover require the appropriate approval.

### Stage I — Support and learn
Record client feedback, defects, accepted changes, and support obligations. Promote only verified, non-sensitive, appropriately scoped lessons into reusable procedures. Do not copy one client's confidential information into another client's context.

## 4. Research report standard

A publishable niche report should define its audience and scope; include executive summary, findings, sources, retrieval dates, meaningful caveats, conflicting evidence, and next steps; distinguish facts from analysis; avoid fabricated statistics or citations; and satisfy a freshness rule appropriate to the topic. Time-sensitive facts must be checked near publication.

## 5. Opportunity record standard

Each lead/opportunity should record:
- business name and public website/contact source;
- date discovered and last verified;
- source URL/evidence for each important observation;
- observed issue or unmet need, stated without exaggeration;
- proposed service fit and reason;
- qualification status and confidence;
- contact-channel eligibility and suppression/opt-out state;
- prior outreach attempts and outcomes;
- next action and approval requirement.

Avoid collecting personal data unrelated to the prospective business relationship. Do not infer private attributes or claim a prospect has approved contact when no valid basis is recorded.

## 6. Service and pricing policy

The system must be configurable; prices should not be invented in prompts. Before sales automation is enabled, the founder needs to define:
- allowed service packages and exclusions;
- pricing method, currency, minimum price, discount ceiling, and tax/fee handling;
- maximum work hours or compute budget per deal;
- delivery timeline rules and capacity checks;
- revision/support scope;
- payment schedule and refund/termination handling;
- commitments the system may make without approval;
- conditions requiring founder review or professional advice.

Until those values are defined, the system may draft an estimate marked “for review” but must not bind the company to it.

## 7. Customer communication policy

- Be transparent and factual about the company and the communication purpose.
- Never impersonate a human if doing so would mislead the recipient; use required AI/recording disclosures where applicable.
- Respect contact eligibility, channel rules, opt-outs, preferences, rate limits, and quiet hours.
- Do not use pressure, fabricated deadlines, false claims, fake testimonials, or misleading guarantees.
- Record a prospect's request not to be contacted and enforce it at the final send/call boundary.
- Do not repeatedly contact a recipient after no-interest or suppression signals.
- Calls/messages that may have been accepted by a provider but have unknown outcomes must be reconciled before retrying.

## 8. Company metrics

Measure real operational outcomes rather than vanity totals:
- research source quality and proportion of claims with evidence;
- freshness and correction rate for published information;
- opportunity qualification precision and verified-issue rate;
- contact eligibility failures and opt-out compliance;
- response rate and qualified-conversation rate where the channel permits measurement;
- proposal approval and acceptance rates;
- delivery cycle time and acceptance-test pass rate;
- rework, escaped-defect, refund/dispute, and missed-deadline rates;
- client feedback and repeat engagement;
- per-task model/tool cost and gross contribution where cost data is available.

A metric must have a documented definition, source query, window, owner, and known limitations. Do not present estimated revenue, projected savings, or inferred conversion as verified financial facts.

## 9. Operating authority

The founder remains accountable for strategy and has final authority over the company's policies. The automation system may exercise only the authority explicitly represented in policy and approval records. Commercial terms, legal commitments, money movement, external publication, and client production handover require a defined authority boundary.
